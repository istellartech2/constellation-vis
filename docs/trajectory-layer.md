# Tabulated trajectory layer

The trajectory layer replays time-tagged position tables (a reconstructed or
simulated launch trajectory, a satellite ephemeris, …) in the 3D scene,
independently of SGP4, optionally with a 3D vehicle model and attitude. Code:
`src/lib/trajectory/` (`types.ts` contract, `parse.ts`, `interpolate.ts`,
`attitude.ts`, `models.ts`, `renderer.ts`). Samples: `docs/examples/trajectory-sample-*.csv`
(synthetic, generated by a simple 2D point-mass model; not real flight data).

## Internal representation

Every file becomes a `TrajectoryTrack` whose samples are **ECEF (Earth-fixed),
km and km/s**, sorted by time, at least two, with exact duplicate times dropped
(first one kept, warning). The scene draws them inside the GMST-rotated
Earth group, mapping ECEF km to scene units as
`(x/6378.137, z/6356.7523142, -y/6378.137)`.

## CSV / TSV

```csv
# name: Synthetic booster (demo, not real data)
# frame: LLA
# epoch: 2026-11-15T18:00:00Z
# model: starship-v3-booster
# attitude: 0, prograde
# attitude: 391.14, vertical, transition=6
# event: 0, Liftoff
# event: 160, MECO / Hot staging
time_s,lat_deg,lon_deg,alt_km
0,25.996900,-97.154600,0.0000
1,25.996900,-97.154600,0.0017
```

- Separator: comma, tab or whitespace (decided from the header row). BOM,
  CRLF and blank lines are tolerated.
- `#` lines are comments. Recognised metadata (case-insensitive key, other keys
  ignored):
  - `# name: <text>` – display name (default: file name without extension).
  - `# frame: LLA | ECEF | ECI` – default: LLA when lat/lon/alt columns exist,
    otherwise Cartesian columns are read as ECEF **with a warning**.
  - `# epoch: <ISO-8601>` – T0 (e.g. lift-off). Base for numeric times and the
    `epochMs` used for T+ readouts.
  - `# event: <time>, <label>` – split on the first comma; time uses the same
    convention as the time column (T+ seconds or ISO).
  - `# model: <key or url>` – 3D model: a built-in key (`starship-v3-booster`,
    `starship-v3-ship`, `generic-rocket`, `satellite-bus`) or a `.glb` URL.
    Without it only the marker dot is drawn.
  - `# mount: <until>, <offset_m>` – upper stage stacked on a lower stage:
    until `<until>` (time as for events, typically staging) the model is drawn
    `<offset_m>` metres further along its own nose axis, so a ship whose file
    repeats the booster's ascent points sits on top of the booster
    (`# mount: 160, 72.3` for Starship V3).
  - `# attitude: <time>, <mode>[, aoa=<deg>][, transition=<s>]` – attitude
    rule (see [Attitude](#attitude)); `mode` is `prograde`, `retrograde`,
    `vertical` or `aoa` (which needs `aoa=`). Time as for events. Bad lines
    are skipped with a warning. Ignored when the file has attitude columns.
  - `# attitude_frame: ECEF | ECI | ENU` – frame of quaternion columns
    (default ECEF, with a warning).
- Column names are case-insensitive; units may be given as a suffix
  (`alt_km`, `alt (km)`, `vx [m/s]`). A column is recognised only when it is
  exactly an alias plus an optional known unit, so e.g. `q_x`, `x_quat`
  and any other unknown column are ignored. An incomplete quaternion
  (e.g. only `qw, qx`) or only one of azimuth / elevation is ignored with a
  warning.

| Role | Aliases | Units (default first) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| time | `time`, `t`, `utc`, `epoch`, `datetime` | `s`; values are ISO strings or seconds |
| latitude / longitude | `lat`, `latitude` / `lon`, `lng`, `long`, `longitude` | `deg` |
| altitude | `alt`, `altitude`, `height`, `h` | `km`, `m` |
| position | `x`, `y`, `z` | `km`, `m` |
| velocity | `vx`, `vy`, `vz` (also `x_dot`, …) | `km_s`, `m_s` |

| attitude quaternion | `qw`, `qx`, `qy`, `qz` or `q0` (scalar), `q1`, `q2`, `q3` | – |
| attitude angles | `att_azimuth`, `att_az`, `heading`, `yaw` / `att_elevation`, `att_el`, `pitch` / `att_roll`, `roll` | `deg` |

LLA input has no velocity. Cartesian velocity is optional (all three needed).

Attitude columns (optional; quaternion wins when both are present), all
converted at parse time to body → ECEF (`TrajectorySample.attitude`):

- **Quaternion** `qw, qx, qy, qz` rotates body vectors into
  `# attitude_frame` (normalised; a zero quaternion is an error). ECEF is
  taken as is; ENU is rotated with each sample's own geodetic lat/lon
  (E, N, U axes); ECI is rotated by −GMST about +Z (`satellite.gstime`, the
  same convention as ECI positions).
- **Angles** (rocket style, local ENU): nose direction from azimuth `a`
  (clockwise from north) and elevation `e` (above the local horizon):
  `n = (cos e·sin a, cos e·cos a, sin e)`. Roll 0 puts the belly (+Z) at
  `(sin e·sin a, sin e·cos a, −cos e)` — straight down for a level nose,
  toward the azimuth for a vertical one (no singularity). Roll is a
  right-handed rotation of the belly about the nose (+X); without a roll
  column it is 0.

Body frame: +X nose, +Z belly (the heat-shield side for Starship; windward
on a belly-first entry), +Y = Z × X.

## CCSDS OEM (KVN)

Detected by a `CCSDS_OEM_VERS =` line. XML OEM is rejected.

- META blocks: `OBJECT_NAME` (first one → track name), `REF_FRAME`,
  `CENTER_NAME`, `TIME_SYSTEM`. A non-`EARTH` centre or a non-`UTC` time
  system only produces a warning (data is still treated as Earth-centred UTC).
- Data lines `epoch x y z vx vy vz [ax ay az]` in km and km/s; accelerations
  are ignored. `COMMENT` lines and `COVARIANCE_START…STOP` blocks are skipped.
- Each segment is converted with its own `REF_FRAME`, then all segments are
  merged, sorted and de-duplicated.
- `ITRF*`, `ECEF`, `ECF`, `EFG` → ECEF. `EME2000`, `J2000`, `GCRF`, `ICRF`,
  `TEME`, `TOD`, `MOD` → ECI (see approximation below, warning). Other frames
  are rejected.

## Time rules

- Timestamps without a zone designator are **UTC** (CCSDS epochs normally
  have no `Z`); parsing uses an explicit regex and `Date.UTC`, never local
  time. Calendar (`2026-02-01T12:00:00[.sss][Z|±hh:mm]`) and CCSDS day-of-year
  (`2026-032T12:00:00`) forms are accepted. A space instead of `T` works only
  in comma/tab files (whitespace-separated files need `T`).
- The time mode (ISO vs numeric seconds) is decided from the first data row.
  Numeric times are seconds relative to `# epoch:`; without it the caller's
  `defaultEpochMs` is used (warning), otherwise the file is rejected.
- Errors name the physical file line (`7行目: 数値として読めない値があります`).
  Messages are Japanese because they are shown in the UI.

## Frame conversion

- LLA → ECEF: `satellite.geodeticToEcf` (WGS84).
- ECI → ECEF: `satellite.gstime` + `satellite.eciToEcf`, i.e. the scene's
  GMST/TEME convention. This is an **approximation** for EME2000/GCRF/ICRF
  input: precession, nutation and polar motion are ignored (tens of km for
  LEO at today's epochs vs J2000 — adequate for visualisation, not for
  analysis). Velocity includes Earth rotation:
  `v_ecef = R·v_eci − ω × r_ecef`, ω = 7.292115e-5 rad/s.
- ECEF → geodetic for readouts: `satellite.eciToGeodetic(r, 0)` (WGS84).

## Interpolation (`interpolate.ts`)

- `trajectoryStateAt(track, t)`: binary search for the segment, cubic
  Hermite in ECEF. Tangents are the sample velocity when given, otherwise a
  non-uniform three-point finite difference (exact for quadratic motion;
  one-sided at the ends). Exact at sample times and C¹-continuous (each
  sample has a single tangent). Returned velocity is the Hermite derivative (km/s),
  `speedKmS` its norm. Returns `null` outside `[first, last]`. Tangents are
  computed on the fly; nothing is cached on the track.
- `sampleTrajectoryPath(track, { maxPoints = 50000, maxTurnDeg = 0.2,
  maxSagKm = 0.001, maxStepMs })`: the drawn path. All source samples plus
  Hermite points added **by shape, not time**: a segment that turns by θ
  (tangent → chord → tangent) over a chord L is split into m pieces with
  θ/m ≤ `maxTurnDeg` and sagitta L·θ/(8m²) ≤ `maxSagKm` (at most 256
  pieces). Straight coasts get nothing; a flip or bank turn tabulated at 1 s
  gets many. If the total would exceed `maxPoints`, both tolerances are
  relaxed together (×1.5 steps) until it fits, so the tightest curves keep
  their share. Flight 15's ship (5 679 samples, 22 h) draws ~47 000 points
  (~1 m chord error); the old fixed cap of 4000 decimated it to every
  ~1.4th sample, so entry and approach curves were drawn as coarse corners.
  Source samples are only dropped (decimated, endpoints kept) when the table
  alone has `maxPoints` rows or more. An explicit `maxStepMs` also caps the
  time step.

## Attitude

`trajectoryAttitudeAt(track, t, state)` (`attitude.ts`) returns body → ECEF.
It is a pure function of time (scrubbing backwards is exact):

1. **Tabulated** (samples carry `attitude`): slerp along the shortest path
   between the two bracketing samples, linear in time.
2. **Rules** (`# attitude:`; none → `prograde`). Each mode is evaluated from
   the interpolated state:
   - `prograde` / `retrograde`: nose along / against the Earth-relative
     velocity;
   - `vertical`: nose along the WGS84 geodetic normal;
   - `aoa`: nose rotated from the velocity toward local up by `aoa` degrees in
     the velocity/up plane, belly facing the flow (`aoa=0` = prograde,
     `aoa=90` in level flight = vertical, `aoa=180` = retrograde; undefined
     while the velocity is exactly vertical, where it falls back to prograde).

   The velocity modes blend (quaternion slerp) to `vertical` as the
   Earth-relative speed falls from 150 to 30 m/s (smoothstep), so a vehicle
   stands on the pad and at a catch. A rule change is a slerp from the
   previous attitude over `transition` seconds (default 8, smoothstep
   weight); a rule at or before the first sample applies immediately.
   Roll: belly = local "down" perpendicular to the nose, plus 0.02 × the
   downrange direction perpendicular to the nose. That small term only
   matters within ~1° of vertical, where it equals the limit of "down ⟂
   nose" for a nose pitching over downrange, so there is no roll snap at
   lift-off. Downrange is the great circle through the first sample and the
   sample farthest from it (a catch returns to the pad, so the last sample
   would be degenerate), evaluated at the current position; it does not flip
   when the horizontal velocity reverses. A nose ~1° off vertical leaning
   *uprange* is a true singularity of this rule (roll swings quickly
   there). No slew-rate limit; transitions are the only smoothing.

## 3D models

`models.ts` registry (`TRAJECTORY_MODELS`): `starship-v3-booster` (72.3 m),
`starship-v3-ship` (52.1 m) — code-made meshes from uchusen-rockets (MIT, see
[third-party](THIRD-PARTY.md)) in `public/models/`, fetched as
`./models/<key>.glb` like the Earth textures — plus procedural
`generic-rocket` (~40 m, cylinder + cone + fins) and `satellite-bus` (shown
as 「標準バス」 in the model selector; box with two wings along body ±Y, 10.4 m
span — also the model of the 「近接」 satellite camera). Any other `model` string is fetched as
a `.glb` URL / object URL.

- Built-in GLBs: metres, glTF +Y = nose, origin at the base on the axis, no
  textures (KHR_mesh_quantization). The ship's tiles are on glTF +Z (bare
  steel on −Z), so glTF +Z = body +Z (belly): body (X, Y, Z) = glTF
  (+Y, −X, +Z).
- Unknown GLBs are normalised by bounding box: longest axis → body X, nose
  toward + of that axis (glTF +Y when Y is longest, the usual "up" of rocket
  models); base = minimum along it at the lateral bbox centre. Roll is
  arbitrary.
- **Position / pivot convention.** For rockets the track position is the
  vehicle's **base** (engine end) at every attitude: the model's geometric
  centre is placed at `track point + nose · L/2`, so the pad/catch contact is
  exact and stages stacked with `# mount:` stay joined. Flips therefore turn
  the vehicle about its base. `satellite-bus` uses its centre.
- **Landmark models** (`LANDMARK_MODELS`, not offered in the 軌跡 panel's
  model selector): `launch-tower` (procedural, ≈145 m lattice column with
  girders and X bracing, a carriage and two catch arms at ~100 m). Its origin
  is the launch-mount axis at ground level (where a vehicle stands and is
  caught); the column stands 22 m toward body +Y and the arms straddle the
  axis ±6.5 m apart along body Z, so a 9 m vehicle on the axis never touches
  it. See [Landmarks](#landmarks).
- Templates are cached at module level by model string (scene rebuilds don't
  refetch). Instances are clones sharing the cached geometry/materials;
  renderers only remove their clones. Built-ins stay cached; user GLBs are
  disposed 10 s after the last track using them is removed.

## Rendering (`renderer.ts`)

- Trails, markers and event dots live in the GMST-rotated Earth-fixed group,
  stored in the overlay-local frame (ECEF as (x/Re, z/Rp, −y/Re), the
  ellipsoid is the unit sphere there). The group is y-scaled by
  `EARTH_FLATTENING` exactly like the globe, so a point at 0 km lies on the
  drawn ellipsoid and the launch-site imagery. (Before, the group was
  unscaled while the globe was flattened: a pad at 26° N sat ~4.1 km above
  the drawn ground, ~8 km off along the meridian.) The vehicle pass applies
  the same flattening when it places models, landmarks and ground discs,
  and undoes it for the camera when it tests Earth occlusion and the lift.
- The bright trail's live tip is the track's Hermite state at the sim time
  (the same curve the path points and the model use), not a chord between
  path points. Each frame only the one or two touched segments are uploaded
  (`addUpdateRange`); a re-base uploads the buffers in full.
  Positions are stored unlifted; each frame every track group is scaled
  about the Earth's centre to lift it radially by `min(0.0001, 0.01 × camera
  distance to the NEAREST visible track tip)` radii — ~0.6 km from afar
  (against z-fighting with the globe), only metres when following, so the
  trail meets the model. One lift for all tracks: a per-track lift moved the
  other track's path by up to ~0.6 km as the camera followed one vehicle.
- **Precision.** Both lines of a track sit in a child group whose origin is
  re-based onto the vehicle when the camera is within ~320 km of it and the
  vehicle is more than ~64 km from the current origin (a rewrite of the two
  segment buffers). Float32 vertices near 1 Earth radius only resolve
  ~0.4 m, which made trails shimmer against the model at follow-camera
  range while the camera moved; relative to a nearby origin they resolve
  millimetres.
- Name labels follow each visible track's marker; event labels are shown for
  the focused track only (`setFocusedTrack`), whose event dots are enlarged.
  Labels are fixed-pixel canvas sprites hidden when the Earth is between the
  camera and the point.
- `renderVehicles(renderer, camera, simDate, rotAngle)` is a second pass after
  the main render: models live in their own scene in metres, placed relative
  to the camera (the camera/vehicle difference is taken in float64 scene units
  before scaling to metres), drawn by a camera with the main camera's
  orientation, fov, zoom and view offset and its own near/far around the
  visible models, after clearing depth. Main-scene depth (near = 0.1 Earth
  radii) cannot resolve fins on a 70 m vehicle, and logarithmic depth would
  z-fight at that scale too. Models smaller than 2 px or behind the Earth
  are skipped; at ≥ 6 px they replace the marker dot (one-frame lag).
  Ground discs are drawn first in their own sub-pass (unlit, loose depth
  range), then depth is cleared again and the models are drawn with near/far
  from the models alone (≈ distance ± length), so close-up depth stays tight.
  Lighting: a directional light from the real sun direction, a sky/ground
  hemisphere fill along the nearest vehicle's local up and a dim neutral
  PMREM room environment (the GLB steel is fully metallic and would be black
  without one), so a night-side vehicle stays readable.
- Name labels sit at the current-frame trail tip, i.e. on the vehicle's base.
- Because models are drawn after a depth clear, they are always on top of the
  main scene (the globe partially hiding a vehicle at the limb is not
  modelled).
- **Ground reference.** Below 10 km altitude a faint disc with a polar grid
  (radius 3 km, fading out from 5 km) is drawn tangent to the WGS84
  ellipsoid at sea level under the vehicle. The globe has no terrain, and its
  standard 128 × 128 sphere has facets up to ~1.9 km below the true surface
  (chord sag `R(1 − cos(π/128))` along a parallel). While riding low, the
  globe is swapped to a finer sphere (see [Earth geometry
  swap](#earth-geometry-swap)), leaving ~0.1–0.2 km; the disc is the actual
  sea-level reference.
- `localStateAt(id, t, pos, vel)` gives the true (unlifted) Hermite position
  and velocity in the Earth-fixed, world-aligned frame before the GMST
  rotation (ECEF / Re as (x, z, −y), i.e. flattened like the globe; the
  follow camera and globe chase use it); `vehicleLengthM(id)` the model length
  (registry length before loading, 50 m without a model).
- **End hold.** `track.holdEndMs` (default 0) keeps the model, marker and
  name label at the last sample for that long after the track ends (a
  vehicle standing on the pad after landing), mirroring the 15-minute pad
  hold before the first sample. Guided tours default it to 30 s per track.
- **Label stacking.** A name label (track or landmark) whose screen box
  overlaps an earlier visible one is drawn one label height lower per
  overlap (e.g. booster and ship during the stacked ascent).

### Trail options

`track.trail` (`TrajectoryTrailStyle`) changes only how the trail is drawn;
changing it never rebuilds geometry (`setTracks` keeps entries whose samples,
events, colour and name are unchanged), so callers can swap it per frame or
per tour chapter.

| Field | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `future` | `true` | `false` hides the faint full path, so only what has been flown is drawn. Event dots ahead of the vehicle are hidden too. |
| `windowMs` | — | The bright trail covers only the last `windowMs` before the vehicle and fades linearly to transparent toward its old end. The faint path is cut the same way behind the vehicle (its part not flown yet is kept, if `future`). Older event dots are hidden. |
| `frame` | `"earth"` | `"inertial"`: every trail point is drawn where it was in inertial space when flown — rotated about the polar axis by ω⊕·(t_point − t_now) inside the GMST-rotated group — so an orbit is a closed ring that stays put while the globe turns under it, instead of an Earth-fixed ground-relative spiral (14 loops of a day-long flight) that whirls around with the Earth at high playback speed. The newest point coincides with the vehicle in both frames. Event dots are hidden (they are Earth-fixed points). |

Implementation: both fat lines carry per-segment `[start, end]` times
(seconds after the first sample); a small patch of three's `LineMaterial`
shader (`patchTrailLineMaterial`) computes each vertex's age for the window
fade and applies the inertial rotation, so neither option costs per-frame
geometry work. If the shader no longer has the expected anchors the patch is
skipped with a warning and trails are drawn as before.

Choosing a frame: `"inertial"` with a window of about one orbit suits whole-
globe views of an orbiting vehicle (the ring is calm and shows the orbit
plane); keep `"earth"` near the ground and when following a vehicle, where
the trail must meet the launch site, the ground track and the landing point.

### Landmarks

`setLandmarks(landmarks)` (scene: `setTrajectoryLandmarks`, param
`trajectoryLandmarks`) draws fixed points of interest with the trajectory
layer (`TrajectoryLandmark`): a marker dot, an optional label (at the top of
the model, if any) and an optional upright model (`model`, e.g.
`launch-tower`; body +X along the local vertical, body +Y toward
`headingDeg`). `fromMs` / `untilMs` limit when it is shown;
`maxDistanceKm` shows the dot and label only while the camera is that close
(the model follows the usual pixel-size rules). Models are drawn by the same
camera-relative metre pass as the vehicles, so a 145 m tower and a 52 m ship
share one depth range.

## Playback, seek and follow camera (`visualization.ts`)

- **Seek.** `SatelliteScene.seekTo(date)` resets the `SimulationClock`
  (`reset(simMs, realMs)`, speed kept) without rebuilding the scene, notifies
  `onSimTimeChange` at once, restarts the selected satellite's ground-track
  trail and orbit line from the new time (no back-filling on forward jumps)
  and forces an ISL recompute. The editor's start time is not touched.
- **Follow ("ride").** One mechanism serves both a trajectory track (追従)
  and the selected editor satellite's 「近接」 camera (see [satellite
  attitude](satellite-attitude.md)). The two differ only in the subject and
  the camera holder: Earth-fixed for a track, the satellite's LVLH frame
  (+X along-track, +Y radial) in 近接, so the satellite sits still and the
  Earth turns below; the 近接 model is drawn with the satellite's own
  resolved attitude (global 通信タブ setting plus any shell / satellite
  override, `attitude/resolve.ts`). For tracks: scene param
  `trajectoryFollowId` (applied in the constructor and live, so it survives
  rebuilds) with `onTrajectoryFollowEnd` when the scene drops it (track
  removed or hidden, a satellite camera mode chosen, a satellite-mode view
  applied); 近接 ends with `onCloseUpEnd` when no editor satellite is
  selected. While riding:
  - the camera holder turns with the Earth (same rotation as the overlay
    group), so overlay coordinates are camera-holder coordinates and the
    ground does not spin;
  - the vehicle state comes from `localStateAt` at the frame's sim time
    (clamped into the span; held on the pad before lift-off) and the camera
    moves rigidly with it, no smoothing; OrbitControls still orbit/zoom about
    the vehicle, with the local vertical as up;
  - zoom limits 0.6 × vehicle length to 2500 km (3× zoom speed); initial
    framing 3 × length away, side-on (perpendicular to the flight direction)
    and 12° above the horizon, so ascent and return are seen in profile;
  - the main camera's near plane shrinks with the follow distance (restored to
    0.1 on exit); country borders / night lights get a polygon offset and the
    cloud shell is hidden while the camera is below it;
  - ground-station markers (64 km spheres) and their cones within 200 km of
    the camera are hidden (and not pickable), so a station at the launch site
    does not swallow the view; they reappear when the ride ends or the camera
    moves away;
  - after a scene rebuild (e.g. a settings change) the same ride resumes
    with the user's orbit offset: `useSatelliteScene` captures
    `getRideOffset()` (camera − target and the local vertical, in holder
    coordinates) before disposal and passes it as `initialRideOffset`; the
    first ride frame rotates it onto the current vertical and clamps it to
    the zoom limits. A ride started by the user still frames from the
    default angle;
  - the saved/persisted view is never overwritten; ending the follow restores
    the previous free-camera framing;
  - guided tours set a framing right after starting or keeping a follow
    (`setTrajectoryFollowFraming`; `setTrajectoryFollow` resets it):
    `distanceM` (applied at once on a new follow, else eased, then the zoom
    is free again), `targetUpM` (orbit target raised along the local vertical,
    e.g. to the middle of a stacked rocket), `includeId` (a second track kept
    in frame: target = midpoint, distance never below what fits both) and
    `transitionS` (easing time constant, 0 = cut).
- **Panel** (表示 → 軌道データ): per track "▶ 開始から" (seek to first sample −
  5 s, follow, 1×, play), "追従" toggle, "全体" (overview, ends follow), model
  selector (built-ins or a local .glb), and a scrub slider (T+ mm:ss) for the
  followed track or the first visible track in span; the live readout shows
  T+, altitude (km) and speed (km/h).
- **Known gaps.** The graticule (lines, no polygon offset) may shimmer near
  the horizon at very close zoom; the detailed globe still has no terrain.

### Earth geometry swap

While riding with the camera below 300 km altitude, every globe layer
(surface, borders, night lights, clouds, atmosphere shells — they differ
only by scale) is switched to one shared high-segment sphere, built lazily;
above 350 km (hysteresis) or when the ride ends the original 128 × 128
geometries are put back and the detailed one is disposed. Free-camera views
never build it.

| Sphere | Vertices | Indices | Sag (parallel / meridian) | Memory (pos+normal+uv+index) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| standard 128 × 128 | 16 641 | 97 536 | 1.92 / 0.48 km | ~0.9 MB |
| detailed 512 × 256 | 131 841 | 783 360 | 0.12 / 0.12 km | ~7.4 MB |
| memory-constrained devices (iPad etc., `isMemoryConstrainedDevice`) 384 × 192 | 74 305 | 440 064 | 0.21 / 0.21 km | ~4.1 MB |

Width and height segments keep equal angular spacing (2π/w = π/h), since the
sag of the 128 × 128 sphere comes from its width segments.

### Launch-site imagery

`src/lib/launchSiteImagery.ts` drapes ~80 km × 80 km Sentinel-2 images (EOxCloudless 2016, CC BY 4.0)
(`public/assets/launch-sites/`, see its README for source, license and
regeneration) over the base map around major launch sites. Each patch is a
64 × 64 lat/lon grid on the WGS84 ellipsoid (geodetic latitude, so the pad
sits on its own imagery), 2 m above it, in a sub-group of the Earth group
(so the geometry swap above and the shell depth bias leave it alone). It is
transparent with `depthWrite` off, `polygonOffset` (−2, −8) and
`renderOrder` −1: it draws after the opaque globe and before the night-light,
border, cloud and atmosphere shells, and never ties with the globe's
vertices (128- or 512-segment). Its shader copies the layered globe's
daylight, dusk tint, night lift and filmic curve (flat under 地球を明るく表示),
and fades the outer 20 % of each side into the base map. It also fades out
with camera distance (fully drawn within 400 km, gone beyond 1100 km), since
from afar the photo would read as a square against the map's colours.

The base map's latitude is sampled by sphere angle (parametric latitude),
up to ~0.1° (~10 km) off geodetic latitude at mid-latitudes, so coastlines
can step slightly at the faded edge; the patch itself is placed correctly.

Loading is lazy and mode-independent (rides, 近接, the free camera zoomed
in): `index.json` is first fetched when the camera drops below 1500 km
altitude, and a site's image when the camera is within 1500 km of it and
below 1500 km (kept up to 2000 km; released 20 s after leaving). The default
whole-globe views request nothing. Only the image-based globes (衛星写真 /
地図（彩色）) draw patches; save-data skips them; memory-constrained devices
decode them at half resolution. While a patch is drawn the scene reports the
credit (`onLaunchSiteImageryCredit`) and the app shows it in the bottom-right
corner, below the playback bar / tour controls.

## Sample data

Both use `# epoch: 2026-11-15T18:00:00Z` (late morning at Boca Chica, so the
models are sunlit).

`docs/examples/trajectory-sample-booster.csv` (1 s spacing, catch at the
launch site ≈T+417 s) and `trajectory-sample-ship.csv` (2 s spacing, SECO
≈T+522 s at ≈145 km / 7.3 km/s, then ≈15 min of suborbital coast down to
60 km). Both share an identical ascent up to hot staging at T+160 s
(≈70 km, 1.6 km/s). They come from a 2D point-mass model in an inertial plane
through Boca Chica at azimuth 97° (spherical Earth, exponential atmosphere,
simple pitch programme, boostback duration solved by bisection); they are for
demonstration only.

Attitude rules in the samples (illustrative, not real flight attitudes):

- Booster (`starship-v3-booster`): `prograde` from 0 (the low-speed blend
  keeps it upright on the pad); at hot staging `aoa=125` over 10 s, a flip
  over the top to a nose-uprange, roughly level boostback attitude
  (`retrograde` would point the nose 45–57° *below* the horizon here, because
  the booster is still climbing steeply); `vertical` from T+190 before the
  horizontal velocity reverses (aoa is undefined when the velocity passes
  through vertical); `retrograde` (engines first) from T+280 for the descent;
  `vertical` at the landing burn (T+391) for the catch.
- Ship (`starship-v3-ship`): `prograde` through SECO and the coast (belly
  down); `aoa=70` from T+1080 (≈110 km) over 40 s for a belly-first entry.
